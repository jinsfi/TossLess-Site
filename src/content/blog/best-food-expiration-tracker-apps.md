## What to look for

### How fast can you add food?

If adding food takes more than a few seconds, you will stop. Check whether the app scans barcodes, lets you pick from common foods, and fills in a use-by date for you.

### How does it remind you?

Some apps send an alert for every item, which can turn into noise. Others send a single daily summary. Think about which you will actually read.

### Where is your data kept?

Some apps keep everything on your phone with no account. Others sync through iCloud or their own servers. On-device is more private. Syncing is better if you share a kitchen across phones.

### What does it cost?

Some apps are free with a paid tier. In FridgeTrack's case, according to its listing, the paid tier is what lets you save items or get reminders. Read the listing's in-app purchases before you invest time entering food.

## How the apps compare

### FridgeTrack

Its listing describes it as offline-first, with no login, and says the food inventory stays on your device. It tracks fridge, freezer and pantry items, including opened and frozen dates. According to the listing, saving items and reminders need the paid Pro tier.

### Food Saver

A one-time $2.99 purchase, according to its listing. It scans barcodes and expiry dates, supports batch scanning, and creates location-based reminders through Apple Reminders. It syncs across your devices with iCloud.

## Common questions

### Do these apps tell me if food is safe?

No app can. Dates and storage guides are estimates for planning. Check the look, smell and texture of food, and follow any date on the package. See our guide to [use-by vs best-before](/blog/use-by-vs-best-before/) for what the dates on labels mean.

### Can I just use a notes app or a spreadsheet?

You can, and some people do. The trade-off is that a notes app won't work out the date for you or remind you at the right time.

### Is a paid app worth it?

Only if the free tier is too limited for how much food you track. Try the free tier for a week first.
